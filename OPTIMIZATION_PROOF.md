## 1. Matrix correctness argument

Let A and B be the exact matrices generated by the reference's seeded random sequence. The reference returns:

`R = (sum_i sum_j ((sum_k A[i,k] * B[k,j]) mod m)) mod m`.

Addition and multiplication respect congruence modulo m, so the intermediate modular reductions can be deferred until the final reduction. Rearranging the finite sums gives:

`R = (sum_k (sum_i A[i,k]) * (sum_j B[k,j])) mod m`.

The candidate generates every entry in the same order: all rows of A, then all rows of B. It accumulates column sums of A and row sums of B and evaluates this expression with exact Python integers. There is no cached answer, approximate arithmetic, changed random generator, or omitted random draw.

This proves the mathematical equivalence for nonnegative dimensions and positive moduli with those input sequences. The implementation explicitly accepts integer dimensions 0 through 2048 and integer moduli 1 through 2^64-1. Its parameter guards are shared by execution and admission, so unsupported inputs are refused before bidding.

Time is O(n squared), because generating the 2n squared inputs is the dominant operation. Additional storage is O(n), ignoring integer bit lengths. The reference performs cubic matrix multiplication.

## 2. Prime-count correctness argument

For every composite integer x below hi, at least one prime divisor is no larger than sqrt(x), hence no larger than `isqrt(hi - 1)`. The base sieve finds all those primes.

For each segment of [max(lo, 2), hi), mark multiples of each base prime p starting at the greater of p squared and the first multiple of p in that segment:

- Every marked integer is composite: marking never begins at p itself.
- Every composite integer is marked: its smallest prime divisor is in the base list, and the composite is at least that divisor squared.
- Consequently, the unmarked integers are exactly the primes in the half-open interval.

The fixed segment size is 262144 entries. Shared guards cap hi at 10^12 and interval width at ten million, bounding the base-sieve allocation as well as the segment allocation. This implementation is not intended to accept arbitrary huge ranges. The production bidder refuses unsupported work before bidding.

## 5. Economic argument and what remains unknown

For the same on-time contract price and unchanged other delays, reducing billed time by delta seconds raises profit by `cost_rate * delta`. Under best-value scoring, reducing an honest estimated delivery time by delta seconds permits a price increase of up to `time_weight * delta` at the same numerical score, subject to the task budget. This is the economic reason to invest in verified speed.

That does not tell us the best price. A 1.28 multiplier over an inaccurate cost estimate can still lose money, while a 1.28 multiplier over a tiny, accurate compute-only cost can leave most of the available revenue unclaimed. Waiting time and network delay must be included in expected billed cost.

The integrated timing model measures n-squared matrix coefficients in two modulus-size buckets. The sieve model measures base-prime/segment setup and interval marking separately. Initial delivery overhead is 0.12 seconds; successful unqueued settlements update it using the recent 90th percentile plus 0.02 seconds. Successful non-hash executions adjust a bounded compute-time bias against the raw model. Hash outcomes do not train a throughput multiplier because their variance comes from the task.

Admission includes queue time, computation, and delivery overhead. New work is refused while the current computation is overrunning its prediction. Quotes match the wire's four-decimal precision. SIGINT/SIGTERM on the tested macOS runtime initiate a graceful stop: refuse new bids and wait for pending bids and awarded work to settle. A real local protocol test requests a stop while a bid is still outstanding and verifies that the subsequent award is delivered.
